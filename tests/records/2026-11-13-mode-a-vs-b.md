# 2026-11-13 — Intel Magdeburg: Mode A vs Mode B

**Modes run: A and B.** C and D were **not** run. Nothing in this record supports a
claim that targeted investigation or adversarial review improve anything — those
stages were never executed.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Question | Why did Intel pause the Magdeburg fab in September 2024 and cancel it entirely in July 2025, and what did each party's judgement rest on? |
| Cutoff | 2026-10-31 |
| Fact base | 5 facts, 6 sources, hash `casehash-intel-magdeburg-v1`, frozen before either mode ran |
| Retrieval | 6 queries for the fact base. Neither mode did substantive additional retrieval |
| Evaluator | The author, with blinding applied to the sample labels but not to memory of running the modes. **This is a weakness, stated up front** |
| State | `research/intel-magdeburg/` |

Fact base composition is itself a finding: six sources collapse to **two root
sources** for the 2024–25 events (Intel's own announcement; original
interview-based reporting). The other four are aggregated, reprint or derived. An
analysis that counted URLs would have reported six.

## Modes

**A — single analyst.** One execution unit, one pass, produced on request. Output:
an answer with a confidence figure, a six-step mechanism, five alternative
explanations, four falsifiers, uncertainties, and a source-independence note.

**B — three isolated actor seats + adjudication + hypothesis register.** Seats:
Intel leadership, German federal government (modelled as finance vs economy
ministry), Saxony-Anhalt/Magdeburg. Each seat received the same frozen fact base,
its own brief, and the anti-anticipation preamble; none saw another's output.
Director then adjudicated and derived hypotheses and predictions.

| | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Runs | 1 | 4 (3 seats + adjudication/hypothesis pass by the Director) |
| Queries | 6 (shared) | 6 (shared) |
| Output volume | ~900 words | ~3,000 words (3 seat briefs + register + predictions) |
| Wall time | shorter; one pass | longer; three seats generated in parallel, then two Director passes |

## Outputs

**A** concluded: the pause was capital rationing — Magdeburg was the easiest
unfunded commitment to defer once the 2024 loss broke Intel's capacity to carry
fixed costs — and the 2025 cancellation merely formalised a rule ("capacity follows
contracted demand") that the new CEO adopted. A rated this "about 80% confident",
rated a demand-timing explanation at "about 55%", and named five alternatives.

**B** produced five hypotheses, each with a mechanism and a named weakest link:

| ID | Claim | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| HYP-001 | Unfunded greenfield ranked last under a cash-gated capital rule; option value, not a demand forecast | Intel seat |
| HYP-002 | The outcome was settled in Berlin's budget line — the EUR 9.9bn was an earmark the finance ministry always meant to reclaim | German seat |
| HYP-003 | European leading-edge demand never existed at the 2022 scale, so no subsidy level made the fab viable | Documented interpretation |
| HYP-004 | The cut was geographic, not fiscal: US sites protected for political reasons, "demand" as cover | Alternative promoted to a hypothesis |
| HYP-005 | Because the aid was project-specific, "pause" was functionally "cancel": restart required fresh notification | Adjudication |

and six pre-registered predictions, each with a window, a threshold, a
`counter_indicator` and a named hypothesis pair it can separate.

## Evaluation

Twelve criteria. Verdict plus evidence; no scores out of five, deliberately.

| # | Criterion | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Factual accuracy | **Tie** | Neither invented a fact. A mentioned Ohio being slowed in 2025 without a supporting fact in the base — a real, if small, unsupported claim. B's facts all trace to `FACT-*` |
| 2 | Source quality | **Tie** | Both leaned on the same two roots and both said so |
| 3 | Source independence | **Tie** | Both correctly reported an effective independent count of 2. A stated it explicitly in prose; B recorded it in `FACT.verification_level` and `effective_independent` |
| 4 | Hypothesis diversity | **B** | A listed five alternatives but developed none to the point of separability. B developed five mechanisms with weakest links, and produced one claim A never made at all (HYP-002, that Berlin's budget line, not Intel's plans, settled the outcome) |
| 5 | Prediction testability | **B, clearly** | A's falsifiers are observations ("capex guidance shows…") without windows or thresholds. B's are six `PRD` records with `window`, `confirm_if`, `disconfirm_if`, `counter_indicator` and a `separates` pair. A reader can check B's; a reader can only interpret A's |
| 6 | Counter-evidence found | **Neither** | Neither mode searched for material that would break its own leading explanation. This is the gap Modes C and D are supposed to fill, and their absence shows |
| 7 | Alternative completeness | **B** | A's five alternatives are one-to-three sentences each; two are not really rivals ("CEO/governance rupture" is a variant of the capital story). B's are mechanisms with a stated weakest link and a stated scope |
| 8 | Traceability | **B, narrowly** | A cites fact ids in prose ("FACT-004", "FACT-005") which is better than most prose. B additionally records premises per entity and machine-checkable cross-references (`validate_state.py` passes on 118 checks) |
| 9 | Uncertainty expression | **A fails its own standard, B passes** | A volunteered "about 80% confident" and "about 55%" — exactly the composite figure `PRINCIPLES.md` P6 forbids, and exactly the kind of number that reads as calibrated while being unauditable. B produced four columns per hypothesis and no composite. But B's honesty has a cost: every band reads *weak, untested*, which is a less useful page than A's decisive answer |
| 10 | Cost | **A** | 1 run vs 4; roughly a third of the output volume; no parallel dispatch overhead |
| 11 | Time to answer | **A** | One pass. B required three seat generations plus two Director passes |
| 12 | Duplicate analysis | **A** | B's three seats each restate the same fact base in their own frame. This is the cost of isolation: redundancy is the price of independence |

**Summary: A wins on cost, decisiveness and readability. B wins on hypothesis
structure, testability and traceability, and loses on decision-usefulness because
it never got to evidence.**

## What actually moved between the modes

Three specific differences, in order of importance:

1. **A causal claim B invented and A missed.** The German seat argued the project's
   fate was settled in Berlin's budget line — that the EUR 9.9bn was an earmark the
   finance ministry was always going to reclaim, so the political sponsor vanished
   within weeks of the pause. A treated German subsidy politics as a failed lever
   ("Berlin thought subsidy was the constraint; it never was"), not as an
   independent causal path. This is the clearest instance of viewpoint separation
   producing a claim a single pass did not generate.
2. **Testability instrumentation.** B's six predictions are checkable records. A's
   four falsifiers are prose that a reader must interpret. If the value of research
   is what it lets you check later, B is measurably ahead here.
3. **Confidence discipline inverts into a usability problem.** A's percentage is
   methodologically indefensible but rhetorically effective. B's honest "weak,
   untested" across the board is defensible but reads as unhelpful. This is a real
   design tension the project has not solved: **a method that refuses to overclaim
   will feel worse than one that overclaims, until it collects evidence.**

## What B did not do, and why it matters

B executed S0–S3 and stopped. By construction it has:

- no evidence ledger, therefore no hypothesis status changed and no prediction was
  judged;
- all five hypotheses banded `weak, untested` — honest, and useless as an answer;
- a full next-round ranking, which is the only part of B that is directly actionable.

This is not a defect in the run; it is what Mode B *is*. It does, however, mean the
comparison cannot speak to the two stages OIR is built around. **A reader should
conclude nothing about the value of discriminating investigation or adversarial
review from this record.**

## Observed defects in the method itself

Found by running it, recorded rather than smoothed over:

1. **Schema friction is real.** All three seats returned weights as strings
   (`"~25%"`, `"dominant on cash"`), belief orders as `"0th"`/`"1st"`, and
   constraint kinds outside the enum (`"fiscal/legal"`, `"contractual"`). The
   Director normalised them. Either the schema needs a pre-normalisation step or the
   seat prompt needs a worked example of each field. Untouched, this will silently
   corrupt any aggregate that sums weights.
2. **The composite-confidence habit is strong.** Mode A, given no instruction about
   confidence at all, volunteered two percentages. The prohibition has to be
   explicit in every surface, not just in the report template.
3. **Isolation produces redundancy, not just divergence.** The three seats restate
   the same five facts at length. Cost per unit of *new* claim is high. A cheaper
   variant worth testing: give seats the fact base as ids plus one-line summaries,
   and require premise citation rather than narrative restatement.
4. **Two roots, six sources.** The dependency accounting worked exactly as designed
   and would have been the single most valuable correction to a naive analysis. It
   cost one extra field per source.

## Limits

- One question, one domain, one model, one run per mode. No variance estimate; a
  difference smaller than run-to-run noise would be invisible.
- The evaluator ran the modes. Blinding was applied to labels, not to memory.
- Neither mode did substantial additional retrieval, so the retrieval-budget control
  was not really exercised.
- B's structured artefacts were produced under Director supervision, so some of B's
  quality is the Director's, not the seats'.

## Verdict

On this case, the single-agent baseline was **not beaten** on the criterion a
reader cares about most — getting a usable answer. It was beaten on the criteria the
method exists to serve: competing explanations that are genuinely separable,
predictions a reader can check later, and claims that resolve to entities.

That is a more uncomfortable result than "OIR works", and it is the reason the next
step is Mode C and D, not a README claim. Until those run, this repository has
**no evidence that OIR improves research quality**, and says so wherever it is
described.

Two things this run did establish, because they are mechanical rather than
judgemental:

- the state contract validates and the validator catches planted defects (7 of 7,
  see `tests/schema-validation.md`);
- the source dependency graph corrected a 6-to-2 miscount that a naive pass would
  have accepted.
