# Why Intel paused Magdeburg in 2024 and cancelled it in 2025

## 1. Answer

The best-supported explanation is that Magdeburg was the unfunded greenfield
commitment with the lowest political cost of deferral when Intel's cash position
broke in 2024, and that the 2025 cancellation formalised a rule — capacity follows
contracted demand — which the deferral had already assumed (**HYP-001**). A second,
compatible path runs through Berlin: the EUR 9.9bn was a project-specific earmark
inside a narrowed off-budget vehicle, and the finance ministry's incentive was to
reclaim it, so the project's political sponsor was withdrawing regardless of Intel's
engineering readiness (**HYP-002**).

**Band: weak — untested.** What would move it up: one capex document showing total
capital expenditure flat or lower while the European site is cut, which is what
PRD-001 tests and what no round of this run collected.

### Dissent

The Saxony-Anhalt seat argued something none of the others did, and it survives into
this report: **no actor in the system has both the means and the motive to restart the
project**, so every explanation that treats the site as a live option is describing a
choice nobody can make. Its strongest case: the subsidy is federal and project-tied,
the state cannot substitute EUR 30bn, and Intel's own rule now requires contracted
demand that Europe has not produced. It was not rejected; it was left untested, because
testing it requires the evidence stage this run did not run.

The German seat's claim is also worth preserving over the Intel seat's framing:
Intel's model treats the German aid as a lever that was never the constraint, while
the German model treats Intel's demand language as cover for a fiscal decision taken
in Berlin. Both can be true at different causal layers, and neither was disconfirmed.
