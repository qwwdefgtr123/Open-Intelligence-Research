# SIM-ACT-001-r1 — Intel executive leadership

> Archived in condensed form. The structured model in `actors/ACT-001.json` is the
> verbatim seat output; this file keeps the load-bearing claims and the closing
> dissent line. Condensation is disclosed rather than presented as the full text.

## 1. Core goal, and greatest fear
Goal: keep Intel a leading-edge manufacturer while stopping the cash hemorrhage
(quarterly loss near USD 10bn, USD 10bn savings target, shares down more than half).
The asset is manufacturing capability; cash is the constraint on keeping it.
Fear: not losing Germany, but being bound to spend tens of billions on capacity with
no buyer — the risk the new CEO named in July 2025. A demand miss is survivable; an
irreversible commitment is not.

## 2. Internal structure
Four blocs. Technology/foundry (~25%): can declare a node not ready, a de facto veto
on any fab decision. Finance/treasury (~35%): gates every commitment, controls
sequencing. Product/client (~20%): wants cash back in CPUs, treats foundry capex as a
tax. Board (~20%): the real veto; removed a CEO in 2024 and will not sanction a second
balance-sheet shock. FACT-001 is already the compromise: funded sites continue,
unfunded sites pause, Foundry separated to keep financing options open.

## 3. Resources and constraints
Deployable: sunk US capacity, Ireland, the German aid package (not Intel's money, and
conditional), the option value of a paused site. Blocking: fiscal exhaustion; the
aid's milestone structure, which makes restart a re-application rather than a
resumption; land, utility and supplier commitments; the reputational cost of being
read as a partner who walks. The asymmetry is decisive — US commitments are already
paid for and politically load-bearing, Germany's are neither.

## 4. Feasible and ruled out
Feasible: pause, cancel, retain land, offer advanced packaging instead of a wafer fab,
seek a co-investor, separate Foundry. Ruled out: building as announced (no demand
case), holding the option indefinitely at full political cost, any new EU greenfield
commitment.

## 5. Reaction function
If Berlin offers more money or equity: no restart of a wafer fab; packaging or a later
node instead. If Berlin demands the EUR 9.9bn back: negotiate against documented sunk
cost, refuse a penalty framed as damages. If the Commission opens a recovery
procedure: argue demand collapse, settle rather than litigate. If a lead customer signs
binding volume: the constraint eases, but Ohio and Arizona absorb it first.

## 6. Beliefs
0th: external foundry demand did not materialise at the 2022 scale. 1st: Berlin will
prioritise not appearing to have been taken in. 2nd: Berlin believes the cancellation
is a negotiating squeeze for more subsidy — that belief is wrong, and Intel cannot
disprove it without spending money it does not have.

## 7. Falsifiers
Intel recommitting capital to Magdeburg or any new EU greenfield fab before 2027. Or
Intel funding Ohio and Arizona at planned pace while still pleading demand
insufficiency — which would show "market demand" is cover for a geographic choice.

The judgement I would defend even if every other seat disagreed: Intel's leadership
treated Magdeburg as an option it could not afford to keep, and every public word
about demand was a polite way of saying the money was better spent where it had
already been spent.
