# SIM-ACT-003-r1 — Saxony-Anhalt and Magdeburg (site government)

> Condensed archive; `actors/ACT-003.json` is verbatim. See disclosure note in
> SIM-ACT-001-r1.

## 1. Core goal, and greatest fear
Lock a ~3,000-job anchor onto the cleared site — Intel or an equivalent — and make the
empty field Berlin's problem rather than Magdeburg's. Fear: the site stays empty
through an electoral cycle, the federal government takes the EUR 9.9bn back, and the
state is left with a field and a 2022 promise.

## 2. Internal structure
The state holds land and permitting pace but has no authority over Intel's capital
allocation. The city holds building permits and can delay, but has no money and cannot
afford a reputation for obstructing investment. The chamber represents suppliers who
want orders now, not in 2028, and might prefer a smaller faster tenant. The public
cannot veto a tenant, only a government. Two real veto points: the city can stall a
substitute project it dislikes, and the public can punish a government for admitting
failure.

## 3. Resources and constraints
Can: supply and remediate land, accelerate approvals, fund training and state-level
supplier incentives, lobby Berlin and Brussels. Cannot: reopen Intel's capital
decision; touch the EUR 9.9bn, which is federal and was approved for the Intel
project; substitute EUR 30bn of private capex; manufacture 3,000 jobs.

## 4. Feasible and ruled out
Feasible: keep the option alive at near-zero cost and refuse "cancelled" language;
push Berlin to convert the aid into a site-level instrument; court a substitute
tenant; pre-emptively reframe as a multi-use technology park; escalate publicly.
Courting a substitute is structurally real and dimensionally unrealistic: the cleared
site and permits have value, but only a handful of buyers can build such a fab and they
are in the same demand trough that produced the cancellation. Ruled out: suing Intel
(no enforceable claim, and it would freeze the residual option); state financing;
demanding a timetable the state cannot enforce.

## 5. Reaction function
Berlin moves to reclaim the EUR 9.9bn: escalate publicly, reframe as a breach of the
2022 eastern commitment, demand a site-specific successor instrument. Intel retains
any residual presence: amplify it, refuse to declare the project dead. A credible
mid-size tenant appears: trade scale for speed, before the electoral cycle. No tenant
by mid-2027: pivot to a multi-use park narrative and define the empty field first.

## 6. Beliefs
0th: the site is a perishable asset. 1st: Intel will not return — the
customer-commitment logic is structural and Germany is its worst cost base; it will
keep the land option free as a bargaining chip. 2nd: Intel believes the state has no
alternative and will keep the site warm, so waiting costs Intel nothing. The absence of
any state statement in the fact base is itself informative: intent had to be inferred
structurally, not from words.

## 7. Falsifiers
Any real Intel capital commitment to Magdeburg after October 2025; the EUR 9.9bn
formally repurposed into an open site fund; a signed alternative anchor of 1,000+
jobs; the state publicly declaring the project terminated.

The judgement I would defend even if every other seat disagreed: this actor's rational
play is to keep the field warm and the blame moving upward, because declaring the
project dead is the only move that costs it more than an empty field does.
