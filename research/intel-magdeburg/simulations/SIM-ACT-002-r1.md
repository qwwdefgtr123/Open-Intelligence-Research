# SIM-ACT-002-r1 — German federal government (composite)

> Condensed archive; `actors/ACT-002.json` is verbatim. See disclosure note in
> SIM-ACT-001-r1.

## 1. Core goal, and greatest fear
Two answers, because there are two principals. Finance ministry: convert a frozen
project into budget headroom — the EUR 9.9bn is an earmark that protects nothing and
costs the consolidation account something. Its fear is the reverse conversion: a
legally binding obligation that outlives Intel's interest in it. Economy ministry:
keep one leading-edge logic fab and its supplier ring in Germany so the subsidy
programme stays credible. Its fear is that Magdeburg reads abroad as a bluff.

## 2. Internal structure
The finance ministry holds the title; the economy ministry holds the programme. The
finance side prevails on *whether cash leaves* — no commitment executes without its
budget line. The economy side prevails on *what an appropriated euro is for*, and can
refuse to sign a reallocation out of its line. That is the September 2024 dispute.
The Chancellery arbitrates only at high salience; in 2024 it did not, and coalition
collapse removed the arbiter entirely. The split outlived the government that
produced it.

## 3. Resources and constraints
Deployable: the earmark, the state-aid approval, disbursement, public attribution.
Withholdable: signature, cash, and the re-notification any re-scoped use needs.
Limits: the aid is tied to a named project in a named place, so it cannot be walked to
another firm without Brussels; the vehicle's legal basis was already narrowed; the
state carries its own stake; and Intel's stated gate was customer demand, not German
money. Money the counterparty did not want is not leverage.

## 4. Feasible and ruled out
Feasible: hold and wait; de-commit into consolidation; re-scope under fresh
notification; convert into supplier and design support; conditional re-confirmation on
firm offtake. Ruled out: raising the offer (answers a demand problem with cash, and no
fiscal room); taking equity (no mandate, not requested); compelling construction (a
grant is not a supply contract).

## 5. Reaction function
Firm date with named offtake: re-sign, but re-paper the guarantee so it is
unwindable. Repeated delay without commitments: the finance side treats it as
cancellation and de-commits. State co-funding: federal share shrinks, project
survives.

## 6. Beliefs
0th: Intel's binding constraint is customer commitments, not subsidy size. 1st: Intel
will let the option lapse and call it demand. 2nd: Intel assumes Berlin parks the
money rather than admit the flagship failed — FACT-004 is the counter-evidence, since
the finance side moved to pull the EUR 9.9bn back within weeks. Intel mispriced the
German seat it was actually facing.

## 7. Falsifiers
The finance ministry voluntarily re-earmarking the money after cancellation. Or the
aid proving already disbursed and irreversible. Or the economy ministry conceding the
claw-back without a fight.

The judgement I would defend even if every other seat disagreed: the EUR 9.9bn was
never a commitment to a fab, it was a bookmark the finance ministry was always going
to reclaim, and Magdeburg's fate was settled in Berlin's budget line long before
Intel's July 2025 announcement.
